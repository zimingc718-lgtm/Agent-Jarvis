6 Compliance with OCP Tenets  
6.1 Openness 
This specification will hopefully serve as the future fundamental base for a 
disaggregated power rack to deliver ±400VDC to a nearby IT rack. The base of the 
mechanical rack follows the Open Rack standard. Currently three companies have 
worked together to provide a high-level overview of the Diablo 400V architecture. The 
goal is to standardize items such as, high voltage connectors and mating interfaces.  
6.2 Efficiency  
Transferring power at a higher voltage is more efficient, reduces I2R losses. This 
disaggregated power rack can be used with multiple generations and SKUs of IT racks; 
a disaggregated power rack can remain in the same location in the datacenter while IT 
racks can be swapped as required. This will ultimately reduce the overall infrastructure 
costs. One objective is to use learnings from this architecture to distribute high direct 
current voltage in the datacenter directly to the IT racks to improve overall end to end 
power distribution efficiency. 
6.3 Impact 
This is the first disaggregated power rack specification. Standardizing the high voltage 
rack architecture will hopefully lead to a common supply chain, provide end to end power 
efficiency gains, and leverage mainstream EV industry technologies in HVDC 
charging/battery infrastructure.    
6.4 Scale 
The fundamental advantage of a disaggregated power rack is scalability in terms of 
rated power, redundancy schemes and backup power.  
6.5 Sustainability 
Disaggregated power rack has the ability to be scaled for various power ratings allowing 
it to be re-used for multiple generations of an IT rack. The IT rack can be updated while 
maintaining the same hardware in the disaggregated power rack, therefore reducing the 
need to scrap power building blocks.

7 Scope 
With increased rack power (from 33kW to 100’s of kW), size and power of AI 
accelerators, and rack/POD size growth, there are significant challenges with 
interconnect density, power delivery and overall system integration.  To solve this, the 
disaggregated power rack (also known as sidecar) provides a flexible and evolving 
infrastructure solution.    
The disaggregated power rack can be scalable and updated to meet various power 
requirements from 800kW to 1MW+ (with the option to parallelize power racks together) 
while keeping the disaggregated IT rack the same. 
The rack shall support different power distribution configuration options. A few examples 
are provided in Figure 1, for illustration purposes, not as a final design solution.

9.1.3 AC Loss (Holdup and ride-trough) 
Holdup notion applies to built-in PSU bulk storage that allows time until other form of 
energy support is engaged and activated to support the rack functions in case of AC 
loss. Separate from holdup, the ride-through capability of the PSU defines running 
without interruptions during voltage dropouts and sags/swells. 
9.1.3.1 PSU ride-through without BBU 
Line Overvoltage: Follow ITIC curve for overvoltage and requires PSU to ride-through 
and not latch-off. 
Line Sag: Follow both ITIC and IEC 61000-4-34 and requires PSU to ride-through and 
not latch-off. 
Minimum holdup without BBU requirement is up to 20msec at 100% loading. Addition of 
system distributed holdup is acceptable, between the AC/DC PSU in the Diablo 400 rack 
and with the downstream DC/DC converter, located outside the rack. 
 
9.1.3.2 PSU ride-through with BBU 
Shall ride-through any duration within the BBU hold up time range. 
The PSU/Shelf hold-up energy/time shall be enough covering the smooth transition from 
AC drop out to BBU ramp up at the worst-case condition, which can be the AC drop at 
the lowest point of bulk voltage during peak load as defined in 9.3.2.    
Holdup with BBU requirement shall be decided per solution specific requirements, 
sufficient to cover transition time. 
 
9.2 Energy Storage Solutions 
The rack is designed to support various storage solutions such as battery backup unit 
(BBU) and capacitor bank energy storage units (CBU). Depending on the user’s 
requirement the number of BBUs and CBUs can be adjusted. 
9.2.1 BBU Option 
Rack shall support BBU options. Design shall support power ranges per electrical 
specification. 
• Optional active management via microcontroller for secure access  
• Optional module interface with a Rack Management Unit -interface standard

Power rack shall have optional design features that enable paralleling multiple racks to

10 Power Rack Building Blocks 
10.1 AC & DC PDU 
The 3 phase AC input and DC ±400VDC HVDC control modules are estimated to take up 
to 15OU of space and described below. 
10.1.1.1 AC PDU Configuration 
The AC PDU takes in the AC power from the overhead busway and outputs power to the 
input of each 3 phase AC/DC PSU rectifier.  
The AC PDU shall be designed to: 
• Shall accept input connection to quantity up to 12x200A cords 
▪ Multiple fuses are placed in the PDU 
• AC input connection with two-hole compression lugs or connectorized 
• Optional AC Panel/Cabinet  
The output of the AC PDU 3-phase power is split into smaller rated cables (ex. 200A to 
4x50A) cables to be routed to various power rectifier shelves to power the 3-phase 
PSUs.

10.1.2  ±400VDC Power Distribution 
The output of the ±400VDC from the power shelves is collected and distributed via a 
vertical busbar spanning the full height of the rack.  The source of the ±400V is 
generated by the power shelf or energy storage devices and is transferred via the rack 
busbar which also is connected to the HVDC Output Protection and Control Modules.   
10.1.3  ±400VDC PDU Output Protection and Control Module 
The DC PDU Module shall be designed to support up to 400kW output, with built in 
manageability, output On/Off, protection and telemetry, and no voltage contact on the 
output cables.  
• The HVDC output module shall attach to the ±400V busbar and contain several 
output connectors that will transfer the HVDC power to the IT rack via cables 
• Multiple output cables per output module, based on two options: 
o Option 1: 50kW per cable, qty as needed to support output power range 
o Option 2: 100kW per cable, qty as needed to support output power range 
• The front of the HVDC Output and Control Module shall contain physical on/off 
switches to turn on/off the corresponding output connector. 
• The HVDC Output module shall contain LED indicators, fuses and relays to protect 
each output connection in case of over current.  
• Output voltage disconnect-each output shall disconnect the voltage on the connector 
before the cable is unplugged from either source or load side and when no output 
cable is installed. 
• Optional active management via microcontroller, for secure access 
• Optional module interface with a Rack Management Unit-interface standard defined 
in chapter 11 (CANBus, Ethernet, etc.)

10.4 ±400VDC to IT rack Distribution Cable 
The ±400VDC power is brought out of the Diablo 400 rack via multiple HVDC cables. 
• Cabled cross links to HVDC/LVDC converter shelf can be a minimum of 63A/50kW up to 
125A/100kW each cable. 
• ±400Vdc cable connectors on both ends shall have LMFB short pins to prevent arcing  
• Each output connector contains at least three power contacts: +400V, Common, -400V; 
one earth contact and two sense contacts (LMFB). ±400V and common are all rated 
from 63A to 125A. 
• Connectors shall have a latching mechanism with an integrated physical switch on the 
output connector, so the cable cannot be disengaged until the switch has turned off the 
power of the load and the source at the time of disengagement. 
• Each output of the ±400V rail shall be individually current limited according to its 
maximum current rating. 
• Voltage drop budget 0.1% at 5m length for HVDC output cables. 
• The respective HVDC cabinet output port shall be de-energized when the HVDC cable is 
unplugged from either source side or load side. The load side input voltage shall drop 
below SELV within the safety specified time period.

12.1 Subsystem Thermal Design Requirements 
• This section covers sub-systems such as PSU, BBU, CBU, etc. 
• Air inlet and outlet temperature difference: Building blocks should be designed to meet a 
minimum value under a specified load profile/range and environmental conditions which 
may be specific to each customer. 
• Thermal margin: Adequate margins should be reserved to de-rated values on all critical 
components. In general, a margin of ≥ 4% of derated temperature rating or ≥ 3°C must 
be met under rated load and elevated or worst-case environmental conditions. If this 
requirement cannot be met, failure conditions should be reviewed with the customer. 
Thermal monitoring: Building blocks shall provide the following parameters via the 
defined communication protocol. These include (but are not limited to) inlet air 
temperature, exhaust air temperature, critical component temperatures, fan speed 
reading(s), fan failure signal(s), etc.

• Airflow containment: Appropriate containment features such as side panels, etc. should

15.1 Average current vs RMS current thermal impact 
Let Icc(u) and Vcc(u) be Random Variables, able to take any value from a universe of 
values (u).  
Let function E{ } be Expected Value, i.e., average, of a given Random Variable being 
operated on. 
 
A = E{Icc(u) at GF} = Iavg 
A+x = I_Pk = E{Icc(u) Worst Case Max}  
  
 
 
 
Implementations of GPUs CPUs Average (Thermal) Power algorithms could measure 
and average the processor power.  Within the processor, the dominant power is the 
Dynamic Power which is linear with Current, i.e., Pdyn = Idyn * Vcc, where Idyn = C * 
Vcc * Freq., the controlled and measured Processor Average Power will render a 
resultant Average Current proportional to Average Power.  
  
On the other hand, power losses in hardware circuitry (such as VRs) are result of current 
over Real Resistance.  This VR average power calculation requires use of I_rms (see 
below).   
  
I_RMS to I_AVG ratio is the VR steady state thermal "gain" or how much thermally 
bigger the VR needs to be when compared to one just designed to I_AVG rendered by 
processor at TDP.
