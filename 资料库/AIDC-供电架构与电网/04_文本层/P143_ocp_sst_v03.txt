2. Compliance with OCP Tenets
2.1. Openness 
This specification aims to establish a common, open framework for Solid State Transformers 
(SSTs) as a power conversion element enabling future LVDC distribution architectures in data 
centers. By defining clear electrical, functional, and interface expectations at the medium 
voltage to DC conversion layer, this work supports interoperability between SSTs, downstream 
OCP aligned power racks, and IT infrastructure. The intent is to encourage multivendor 
innovation while reducing integration friction through transparent requirements, well defined 
boundaries, and alignment with open standards and OCP design principles. 
2.2. Eﬀiciency 
As the focus of this specification, Solid State Transformers, enable higher eﬀiciency power 
delivery by combining medium voltage power conversion and direct DC output. By integrating 
advanced power electronics, modular architectures, and optimized control, SSTs support high 
conversion e
ﬀiciency across a wide operating range, contributing to reduced electrical losses 
and improved overall data center power eﬀiciency. 
2.3. Impact 
Besides introducing breakthroughs in technology, standardizing SST functional and 
performance expectations has a significant impact on data center power architectures by 
enabling new levels of density, and integration. A common SST specification, formed through 
iterative e
ﬀorts among key authors, starts from concept, matures over multiple revisions, 
enables a broader supplier ecosystem and encourages feedback for improvements. It 
accelerates adoption of DC centric architectures, and creates a clearer pathway for coupling 
energy with server racks that enable the next generation of intelligence infrastructure. This 
alignment can reduce bespoke designs, improve supply chain scalability, and enable the reuse 
of power infrastructure across multiple data center generations in a long term vision. 
2.4. Scale 
This specification supports scaling across multiple power levels and deployment sizes, from 
distributed or block redundant systems supporting multi-megawatts loads with predictable 
reliability and availability metrics.

2.5. Sustainability 
This specification serves as the foundation for enabling higher density data center power 
distribution architectures through the integration of silicon power electronics semiconductors 
based products called Solid State Transformer. By transitioning away from traditional copper 
and iron transformer technologies, it removes long-standing material and design constraints, 
fostering a more agile and e
ﬀicient power ecosystem. Furthermore, these advancements 
directly contribute to the evolution of the renewable energy industry, facilitating the 
integration of sustainable power sources and supporting the long-term vision of green data 
center infrastructure. 
3. Scope
This document serves as the technical design specification for the Solid State Transformer (SST), 
which is one of the building blocks for the Direct Current power distribution system in the Data Center 
industry.  
Within the scope of this specification, a Medium Voltage Solid-State Transformer (MVSST) is defined as 
a megawatt-scale power electronic converter, which when coupled with energy storage could 
function as a Medium Voltage Uninterruptible Power Supply (MV-UPS). The MVSST itself replaces 
conventional 50/60Hz line-frequency Medium Voltage transformer, rectifier units and energy storage 
(as required by architecture). Among all engineering solutions to fulfill high-e
ﬀiciency AC-to-DC 
conversion, SST is the most strategically innovative approach to enable downstream Low-Voltage 
Direct Current (LVDC) distribution to the IT rack interface. Beyond basic transformation, the SST 
provides advanced grid-interactive capabilities such as programmable MVAC response compliant with 
evolving grid codes, controlled LVDC voltage regulation for power distribution and IT rack 
requirements, and high-density data center design to enable power-dense racks (hundreds KW to MW). 
By utilizing medium-to-high-frequency magnetic components/transformers (MFT/HFT) and 
Wide-Bandgap (WBG) semiconductors such as Silicon Carbide (SiC) or emerging Gallium Nitride (GaN) 
potential, SST targets to achieve superior power density and o
ﬀers a platform for future innovations in 
power distribution system design. 
This document defines the product using a "black-box" approach, with the focus on interfaces as 
application level guidance (e.g. electrical, mechanical, thermal, environmental, control, 
communications, and behavior expectation etc). Vendors shall take the specification as a product 
framework to fill with engineering solutions to hit the design target. 
A few examples for  application architectures for SST are illustrated in Appendix A. The current scope of 
this specification is defined according to the dotted frame in the architecture graph referenced.

4. Base product specifications 
4.1. Product internal compositions 
Figure 1. Main compositions of SST product (not proportional for physical size) 
Component #1: Power conversion. Serving as the core engine of the solid state transformer, this 
component shall be responsible for delivering below key MVAC-LVDC power conversion functions to 
meet the performance specification described in this document. It combines power electronic 
semiconductors, magnetic components, capacitors, PCBA, etc, all integrated within supporting 
thermal and mechanical envelopes. 
Component #2: MVAC input. Serving as the energy gateway of the solid state transformer, this portion 
shall be responsible for o
ﬀering protections to SST from unexpected MVAC transient event and internal 
fault, supporting MVAC power quality requirements for filtering, as well as enabling operational 
sequence for SST state machine transitions. 
Component #3: LVDC output. Serving as the LVDC power distribution entrance and switchboard from 
the solid state transformer, this portion shall be responsible for output port configurations, 
terminations and switchgears for distribution design and installation / servicing requirements.  
Component #4: Firmware, control and communications. Serving as the “neuron” layer of the solid 
state transformer, this portion shall be responsible for component coordinations, system behavior 
control, and the management of both internal and external operational data exchange.

5.1.2. Voltage Ride Through (VRT) 
Voltage ride through requirement in this section applies to conversion and energy storage systems 
between MVAC to LVDC - example structures such as: 
- SST and ESS operating in conjunction, with ESS size dependent upon upstream energy system 
continuity, to a joint LVDC bus with coordinated secondary layer of control, and deliberate 
power sharing performance design target.
- integrated SST and ESS as a single product SKU, functioning as an “MVUPS” application. 
There has not been a unified standard on the LVRT design criteria. This part of the design specification 
is not intended to drive a consensus, but provide guidance on the coordination between SST and ESS 
converter for power electronics design and control; when it comes to amount of energy required to 
“ride-through” the curves defined by di
ﬀerent local utility authorities, ESS capacity can be sized 
diﬀerently and integrated as part of the product SKU at times of deployment (aka. product 
specification). 
After surveying relative utility study authorities on the grid-code definitions, ERCOT [1],  Fingrid, ITIC 
(classic PSU ride-through), IEEE1547 [2] (DER reference), IEEE2800 [3] (DER reference at transmission 
level), MISO, and other utility authorities, are pictured in the section below as reference. Early versions 
of the SST specification prioritize most recognized utility operator authorities defined LVRT definitions. 
Zoom in on the definition of SST’s behavior, with ERCOT graph as illustration -  grey zones in the figure 
below translate to SST’s behavior expectations at times of transient and permanent voltage quality 
conditions.  
- In the OVRT range (1.1pu < x < 1.2pu), SST shall be fully functional and supply 100% of the 
rated output load.
- In the acceptable power quality range (0.9pu < x < 1.1pu for ∞), SST shall be fully functional 
and supply 100% of the rated output load.

5.3.  MVAC Load Acceptance and Rejection 
Multiple types of energy sources exist in the MVAC energy plane as below categories. They oﬀer 
diﬀerent ramp rates when it comes to load acceptance and rejection. 
- utility
- fossil fuel based on-site generation
- renewable energy
Current data center infrastructure has ramp rates defined as approx. 20~50MW/min for utility, TBD 
MW/s for gensets (fossil fuel based) and TBD MW/s for renewable energy. These data are selected 
based on knowledge of existing data center energy infrastructure, which are subject to updates when 
new results become available. Coordination between SST and ESS shall be designed with the utility 
ramp rate as reference for system control design target.

7. System behavior
7.1. State machine and transitions 
Abbr. Status Description 
S0 OFF SST shall be in this state when “freshly getting oﬀ the dock” . 
Sb Standby 
Auxiliary power on, and control system alive with visibilities into the status for 
the gear. The system shall park in this state, when:  
a) SST provisioning process is done and no fault discovered. 
b) SST getting ready to be powered down. 
Whichever scenario, SST is ready to proceed to the next state. 
Sp Precharged 
This is an interim status for SST . It means internal energy storage is charged, and 
ready to convert power with little to none turnaround time. The source of 
precharge can come from either MVAC precharge circuit or ESS for self start. 
S1 ON SST is healthy. It is in steady state operations and converting power. SST’s energy 
source is MVAC. 
S2 FAUL T 
SST is not healthy. It stops converting power, with aux control power staying on. 
Compared with a similar state of Sb, the system is parked with fault messages. 
System control logic shall prevent it from powering back on.  
S3 WARNING 
SST is outputting power, but operating under one of the below states: 
A. Running on ESS - SST transitions back to S1 when MVAC recovers. 
B. It loses redundancy, or operates in a marginal/derated state. It still converts 
power, but with warning messages. 
a) If the failure category is permanent (such as module hardware failure, or 
key component approaching degradation threshold but not yet failed), 
system control logic shall prevent the state machine from transitioning 
back to S1. Such failure shall be manually validated, serviced on, and 
issued with reset with data center operator’s intervention, and SST shall 
go through a new round of provisioning before putting back to 
operations. 
b) If the failure category is temporary (such as overtemperature warning 
OTW, or overcurrent warning OCW), system control logic shall allow state 
machine transition back to S1, when such conditions and system 
recovered back to acceptable operating conditions. SST control system 
shall record the warning condition log, ready for data center operations 
automation hub to scrutinize. 
c) SST can stay in this state for a brief period of time during overcurrent 
protection, operating with constant power characteristics for transient 
response. SST shall transition back to S1 when the downstream fault gets 
cleared.
