What is the risk to the Bulk Electric System (What Bulk Electric System (BES) reliability benefit does the 
proposed project provide?): 
This project will help address the risks posed to the Bulk Power System (BPS) by the disturbance response 
of computational loads, which have reduced or interrupted demand in response to transmission faults.

NERC's Reliability and Security Technical Committee (RSTC) established the Large Load Task Force (LLTF), 
now the Large Loads Working Group (LLWG), to analyze the reliability impacts of emerging large loads. The 
LLWG identified the lack of predictable ride -through performance for large loads as a gap in the current 
NERC Reliability Standards in its Assessment of Gaps in Existing Practices, Requirements, and Reliability 
Standards for Emerging Large Loads white paper (March 2026). NERC issued a Level 2 Alert  in September 
2025 addressing rapid, major load swings  and suggesting changes to interconnection requirements, 
modeling practices, and operation readiness. NERC also issued  a Level 3 Alert  in May 2026 containing 
essential actions for the reliable integration of computational loads. Project 2026-02 Computational Loads 
was initiated in March 2026 to address in the near term the most critical issues associated with integrating 
computational loads.  
 
Project 2026- 02 scope addresses computational load interconnection and modeling, operational 
coordination and information , protection and control systems , and event analysis information . Ride -
through and post -fault active power recovery (PFAPR ) performance requirements are technically distinct 
from that near -term scope and rest on the event analysis and dynamic simulation basis set out in the 
preliminary LLWG and Event Analysis Subcommittee (EAS) white paper Large Load Disturbance Performance 
- Impact Analysis and Ride -Through Recommendations . The Project 2026 -02 project is also facing a 
regulatory deadline of December 31, 2026 for filing with the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission.  This 
SAR is submitted so that the needs associated with disturbance performance, as the next most high-priority 
issue associated with computational load integration, receive a dedicated drafting effort. 
 
The following event records establish the BPS impact of these loads and the technical justification for a SAR 
on this matter.  
 
- On July 10, 2024, the failure of a lightning arrestor on a 230 kV line in the Dominion Energy Virginia 
(DEV) service territory produced a single -line-to-ground fault, that ultimately led to a line lockout 
after a staggered 3-attempt automatic reclosing sequence at each terminal (i.e., 6 fault applications 
within approximately 82 seconds). While, n o data center was disconnected by the fault itself  or by 
the reclosing sequence , approximately 1,500 MW of data center load nevertheless transferred to 
backup generation through customer -side controls and protection and remained off the BPS for 
several hours, producing a measurable frequency deviation, a change in area control error, and a 
localized overvoltage condition that persisted at several substations until DEV system operators 
energized shunt reactors and removed capacitor banks from service. This load transfer is considered 
to be customer-initiated load reduction (CILR).

- In the Texas Interconnection, ERCOT has analyzed 32 large load ride-through events with CILR of 100 
MW or greater between January 2023 and April 2026 and  has indicated that the simultaneous loss 
of 3,200 GW of load under certain system conditions may pose significant risk to Interconnection

- AEP has experienced two significant data center CILR events. In February 2025, a 428 MW CILR event 
occurred. In March 2025, a 227 MW CILR event occurred. More information on the ERCOT events , 
the AEP events, and the DEV events can be found in the preliminary white paper Large Load 
Disturbance Performance – Impact Analysis and Ride-Through Recommendations. 
 
Moreover, the observed response is not consistent between computational load sites or between events at 
the same site. Sites connected to the same substation and exposed to nearly identical voltage depressions 
have behaved differently. For example, a single site reduced approximately 20% of its load at a minimum 
phase voltage of 0.7205 pu during one event and its full load at a shallower minimum phase voltage of 
0.7538 pu during another. Planning Coordinators (PC), Transmission Planners (TP), and Balancing 
Authorities (BA) therefore cannot presently predict how much load a credible fault will remove from service. 
 
PRC-024-4 establishes frequency and voltage protection settings for synchronous generators, Type 1 and 
Type 2 wind resources, and synchronous condensers, and PRC-029-1 establishes ride-through requirements 
for inverter-based resources (IBR). Neither applies to load, and no NERC Reliability Standard establishes a 
minimum voltage ride-through (VRT), frequency ride-through (FRT), or PFAPR performance requirement for 
computational load. 
Purpose or Goal (What are the reliability gap(s) or risk(s) to the Bulk Electric System being addressed, and 
how does this proposed project provide the reliability-related benefit described above?): 
The goal of this project is to address the reliability risk posed by the computational loads performing CILR 
by defining minimum ride -through, rate -of-change-of-frequency (RoCoF) withstand, and PFAPR 
performance requirements to ensure the stability of the BPS. When the evidentiary record of BPS CILR 
disturbances is considered in light of the growing queues of computational loads seeking interconnection, 
it highlights that it is essential to address  issues associated with disturbance performance to support 
integration of computational loads in a way that avoids placing the reliability of the BPS at risk.  
 
To mitigate the risks, two actions are needed: (1) revisions to the NERC Glossary of Terms Used in NERC 
Reliability Standards (NERC Glossary of Terms) to define load ride-through and PFAPR, consistent with the 
computational load and Computational Load Entity definitions under development in Project 2026-02; and 
(2) the development of one or more Reliability Standards establishing minimum voltage ride-through (VRT), 
frequency ride-through (FRT), RoCoF withstand, and PFAPR performance requirements for computational 
loads. 
 
Establishing these disturbance performance requirements will ensure that applicable computational loads 
have clear performance requirements to meet or exceed, while preserving the ability of the T ransmission 
Owner, D istribution Provider , and Generator Owner , in coordination with other applicable entities as 
needed, to establish more stringent performance requirements through their interconnection

Project Scope (Define the parameters of the proposed project): 
The scope of this project shall include: 
• Develop new or modified Reliability Standards addressing computational load ride-through, PFAPR, 
RoCoF withstand, and current consumption during disturbances. 
• Developing terms and definitions for inclusion in the NERC Glossary of Terms that support Reliability 
Standards addressing computational load ride-through and PFAPR. 
• Considering the LLWG and EAS white paper Large Load Disturbance Performance: Impact Analysis 
and Ride-Through Recommendations (preliminary version available on the NERC website); the Level 
3 Alert Essential Actions; the requirements published or proposed by ERCOT, SPP, MISO, Southern 
Company, DEV, AESO, and Fingrid; and other information and stakeholder feedback; develop one or 
more Reliability Standards establishing minimum VRT, FRT, RoCoF withstand, PFAPR , and 
disturbance current consumption requirements for computational loads. 
• Determining applicability, including the point of measurement, the treatment of cooling load, the 
performance verification and disturbance monitoring data required to demonstrate compliance, 
and a tiered applicability and effective date structure addressing sites that are already operational 
or that hold executed interconnection agreements. 
• Addressing the coordination of computational load ride -through obligations with the generation 
ride-through obligations applicable to co -located generation sharing a point of common coupling 
(PCC). Determining if single net PCC  performance region should replace separate load and 
generation ride-through obligations at sites where load and generation are tightly integrated. 
 
Detailed Description (Describe the proposed deliverable(s) with sufficient detail for a drafting team to

This drafting team will develop definitions for inclusion in the NERC Glossary of Terms  supporting the 
requirements described below, including a term for load ride -through and a term for PFAPR. The  drafting

rather than modification of the existing Ride -through definition approved with PRC-024-4 and PRC-029-1. 
In addition, the drafting team should modify or develop  one or more Reliability Standards to address the 
following disturbance performance and mitigation requirements.

• Voltage Ride -Through: Establish minimum VRT performance requirements  applicable to 
computational loads, including requirements for  low-voltage and high -voltage conditions. 
Requirements should define the voltage -time performance envelope ( e.g., expressed as a table of 
voltage magnitudes and corresponding minimum ride -through durations ) applicable to 
computational loads to support BPS reliability during system disturbances. The LLWG recommends 
consideration of the materials above, and recommends  drafting team consideration of the ERCOT 
Voltage Ride-Through Requirements for Large Computational Loads from ERCOT NOGRR 282 as the 
minimum baseline, modified to extend the obligation across the full duration of credible events and 
fault clearing times , and modified to reduce the number of discrete voltage -time steps . Table 1 
provides a recommended starting point for these modifications.  The drafting team should also 
address the applicability of VRT requirements to cooling load providing some flexibility where a site 
demonstrates through study that its cooling configuration cannot practically meet the requirements 
and the residual risk is acceptable for BPS reliability.

< 1.15 and ≥ 0.90 Continuous operation region Continuous 
0.80 < V ≤ 0.90 Mandatory operation region 2.00 
0.50 ≤ V ≤ 0.80 Mandatory operation region 0.50 
V < 0.50 Mandatory operation region 0.25 
*Mandatory operation region would need to clarify if the computational load would need to stay 
connected, but may reduce active power proportional to voltage drop. 
 
• Multi-Disturbance Ride-Through: Establish requirements that address the survivability of successive 
voltage depressions rather than a single fault application. Where a site uses protection logic that 
counts voltage dips within a predetermined window, the counter threshold should accommodate 
the automatic reclosing sequence expected on the tra nsmission system serving the site, provided 
each individual event falls inside the applicable ride-through region. 
• Frequency Ride -Through and RoCoF Withstand:  Establish minimum FRT and RoCoF withstand 
performance requirements at the Interconnection level, considering the IEEE 2800-2022 and PRC-
029-1 criteria. The drafting team should consider whether the duration associated with the high-
frequency requirement requires adjustment so that generation and load do not disconnect 
simultaneously during an over-frequency excursion. The drafting team should also consider existing 
under-frequency load shedding (UFLS) schemes to ensure that FRT requirements do not interfere 
with UFLS schemes. 
• Post-Fault Active Power Recovery:  Establish requirements related to the restoration of load 
following a system disturbance , including restoration magnitude  and recovery timing. The LLWG

recommends a default recovery level of at least 90% of pre -disturbance consumption with entities 
being able to modify the recovery level and time based on system studies considering issues such as 
system strength, stability studies, and fault- induced delayed voltage recovery and other local area 
load dynamics. The LLWG also recommends that the requirement only appl ies to a site whose PCC 
voltage remained inside the ride-through region, and not a site whose PCC voltage fell outside that 
region and that consequently disconnected. 
• Current Consumption:  Establish requirements for the maximum current consumption of the 
computational load site during a disturbance.  
• Applicability and Effective Dates: Develop criteria for applicability and implementation timelines, 
including consideration of both new and existing computational load sites. For comparison, ERCOT 
NOGRR 282 provides a tiered model, in that it establishes a grandfathering date associated with 
operational status or execution of an interconnection agreement and then applies a mandatory 
compliance date to sites satisfying interconnection requirements after a later date.  Computational 
load sites built before the grandfathering date  must conform to the performance requirements 
unless they provide documentation of the technical inability to comply with said  performance 
requirements. Every documented CILR event to date involved sites that were already operational, 
and a framework applicable only to sites entering the interconnection queue after the effective date 
would leave the identified risk unaddressed for the service life of the existing fleet. 
 
Recommended Approach 
The LLWG recommends the drafting team pursue the development of new or modified Reliability 
Standard(s) (with associated Glossary updates)  addressing the disturbance performance requirements for 
computational loads, in the manner of PRC-029-1 for IBRs. 
 
The drafting team is not bound to the LLWG's recommended approach and may revise it or develop an 
alternative approach as informed by its expertise, technical information or analysis, or stakeholder 
feedback. 
 
Technical Foundation and Justification 
The technical foundation document for this project is the LLWG  and EAS white paper Large Load 
Disturbance Performance: Impact Analysis and Ride-Through Recommendations. The preliminary version of 
this document is posted on the NERC website. This white paper is planned to be finalized and published in 
December 2026  and is intended to help inform next steps (including any drafting team efforts) . This 
document provides the event analysis, the review of published and proposed requirements across North 
American and international jurisdictions, and the recommendations on which the deliverables above are 
based. 
 
Additional technical justification can be found in the LLWG Characteristics and Risks of Emerging Large Loads 
and Assessment of Gaps in Existing Practices, Requirements, and Reliability Standards for Emerging Large 
Loads white papers, as well as the LLWG Computational Loads Standard Authorization Request Roadmap.

Cost Impact Assessment, if known (Provide a paragraph describing the potential cost impacts associated 
with the proposed project):  
Exact costs for this project are unknown and are expected to vary by entity, by region, and by site design 
and vintage. Near-term costs are likely to be modest for sites designed after the requirements are known, 
as ride -through capability is largely a fun ction of uninterruptible power supply (UPS) configuration, 
protection and control settings, disturbance counter logic, and transformer winding configuration, all of 
which are established during site engineering rather than through retrofit. Costs are expected to be higher 
for sites already in operation and for cooling infrastructure, which is typically not supported by the UPS and 
may require extension of that capability to pumps, fans, and chillers.  
Please describe any unique characteristics of the BES facilities that may be impacted by this proposed 
standard development project (e.g., Dispersed Generation Resources): 
This project proposes establish ing performance requirements applicable to computational loads , and 
coordination obligations for entities who interface with such users. Other equipment at the computational 
load site may also be affected.

Are there alternatives (e.g., guidelines, white paper, alerts, etc.) that have been considered or could meet 
the objectives? If so, please list the alternatives with the benefits of using them. 
The LLWG has published two white papers and a Reliability Guideline addressing emerging large loads, NERC 
Event Analysis has published two incident reviews, and NERC issued a Level 2 Alert in September 2025 and 
a Level 3 Alert  in May 2026. Requirements published or proposed outside NERC  are already producing 
design change, including ERCOT NOGRR 282, the SPP High Impact Large Load framework , the MISO large 
load proposal , and the Southern Company  and DEV interconnection requirements . T he ERCOT 
requirements in particular have given equipment manufacturers a target to design toward. IEEE 2800-2022 
provides the FRT and RoCoF withstand basis adopted here but addresses IBRs rather than load. Grid- side 
mitigations (e.g., dynamic reactive support, faster protection, modified reclosing practice) reduce the 
severity of the disturbances that trigger CILR and remain necessary for residual risk. 
Collectively these measures represent an important start, but they are voluntary or partial in coverage, the 
envelopes and recovery windows differ between regions, and none establishes an enduring performance 
requirement applicable to sites interconnecting  in future years. Full mitigation requires a Reliability 
Standard establishing minimum ride-through and PFAPR performance.
