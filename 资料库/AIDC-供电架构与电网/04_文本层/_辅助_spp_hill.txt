PURPOSE AND INTENT 
This document outlines binding requirements for all new High Impact Large Loads (HILLs) interconnecting to the Southwest Power Pool (SPP) Transmission System and outlines reliability expectations for HILLs. These expectations and requirements are subject to future technical refinement, stakeholder approval, or incorporation into other SPP governing documents.
BACKGROUND 
With the rapid growth of HILLs - such as data centers, industrial facilities, and crypto mining operations, SPP anticipates a growing risk to system stability during voltage and frequency disturbances. Historically, such loads have not been expected to ride-through transmission faults, largely because their equipment was not designed with this capability.
Today, there are no industry standards explicitly requiring load ride-through performance requirements. However, given the increasing concentration and size of HILLs coupled with observations of uncontrolled load tripping during voltage sags/swells or frequency events may cause or exacerbate cascading outages.
Therefore, it is necessary to develop expectations for load performance like those already required of generators interconnecting to the SPP transmission system.
APPLICABILITY 
The following criteria define a HILL for purposes of these requirements:
10 MW or more if connected to the Transmission System at a voltage level less than or equal to 69 kV, or
50 MW or more if connected to the Transmission System at a voltage level greater than 69 kV. 
The requirements set forth herein shall apply exclusively to HILLs submitting interconnection requests on or after [Insert Effective Date of RR696]. HILLs with interconnection requests submitted to Transmission Owners prior to this date shall make reasonable efforts within capabilities of their facilities to maximize ride-through performance and provide documentation outlining such efforts upon request. When a Transmission Customer has a paired inverter-based resource (IBR), synchronous generator, type 1 or type 2 wind resource, or synchronous condenser electrically connected with a HILL behind a common Point of Interconnection (POI), both the HILL and the resource supporting the HILL shall comply with these performance requirements.
RIDE-THROUGH PERFORMANCE EXPECTATIONS  
4.1Ride-Through Performance for HILLs
Based on whether HILLs are paired with a generator or Electric Storage Resource (ESR), or operate as standalone systems, the ride-through requirements for HILLs and resources can be categorized into the following categories:
4.1.1Standalone HILLs 
Voltage Ride-Through  
HILLs should ride through the voltage profiles shown in Table 1 and Figure 1. These profiles align with the ride-through curves defined by the Information Technology Industry Council (ITIC)—formerly the Computer & Business Equipment Manufacturers Association (CBEMA)—while also considering the voltage ride-through requirements proposed by the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT). Additional details are provided in Appendix A. 
Under Area B, the HILL is expected to meet the following performance requirements:
It is preferable for the HILL to continue consuming power from the grid during shallow voltage sags between 0.5 and 0.8 pu, with load reduction proportional to the voltage dip.
For deep voltage sags in which the rectifier can no longer deliver active power (e.g., less than 0.5 pu), the load may fully transition to the uninterruptible power supply (UPS). The load shall return to at least 90% of its pre-disturbance consumption from the grid after 5 cycles of voltage recovery (0.9 pu or greater).
The load shall not operate in constant power mode in this region; instead, it should operate in constant current mode with reasonable current limitation (TBD).

4.2Ride-Through Performance for Reources Supporting a HILL Behind a Common Point of Interconnection
In this section, the ride-through requirements from IEEE 2800-2022, or PRC-024-4 are considered applicable to IBRs and non-IBRs supporting HILL facilities that are electrically coupled with one another at a common bus. Since the resources and the HILLs share the same POI, they shall coordinate their ride through performance to ensure system reliability and stability during grid disturbances, preventing adverse interactions between the HILL, onsite generation, and the bulk power system.
4.2.1IBRs Paired with HILLs
The IBR Plants paired with HILLs shall adhere to IEEE 2800-2022 Clause 7 requirements, except for subclauses 7.2.1 (voltage protection), and 7.3.1 (mandatory frequency tripping). Where future NERC reliability standards require more stringent ride-through requirements compared to IEEE 2800-2022 Clause 7 requirements, those standards shall supersede the standards set forth in this Section. 
Within the adopted IEEE 2800-2022 Subclauses, the following requirements shall be incorporated as Transmission Provider and Transmission Owner decision points noted in IEEE 2800-2022.
Subclause 7.2.2.1: The Transmission Owner shall specify the nominal voltage for use by this clause. Nominal voltages may include nominal transmission system voltages that are different from standard ANSI C84.1-2016 voltages.
Subclause 7.2.2.2: If the IBR Plant cannot deliver both active and reactive power due to its current limit (or apparent power limit), when the Applicable Voltage is below 95%, then preference shall be given to reactive power while minimizing any reductions in active power.
Subclause 7.2.2.3: The IBR Plant shall give preference to reactive current. In addition, the IBR Plant shall minimize reductions in active power while carrying out the reactive current priority mode response.
Subclause 7.2.2.3.4: The Interconnection Customer shall submit the IBR Plant controls default relationship between the magnitude of incremental positive and negative sequence reactive currents. The Transmission Provider and Transmission Owner, through High Impact Large Load Generator Interconnection Assessment (HILLGIA) Agreement, may specify a required IBR Plant response relationship between the incremental positive and negative sequence reactive currents.

4.2.2Synchronous Generators, Type 1 and Type 2 Wind Resources, or Synchronous Condensers Paired with HILLs  
The ride-through requirements from PRC-024-4 are applicable to synchronous generators, Type 1 and Type 2 wind resources, and synchronous condensers supporting a HILL and are electrically coupled with one another at a common bus.

Control Methodology for Power-Electronic Loads 
For HILLs consisting primarily of power electronic devices and/or variable-speed drives:
Must use constant current control during disturbances;
Must avoid constant power control, which can result in increased current draw during voltage sags and exacerbate local or system-wide instability; and
Must be capable of remaining connected to the system for a minimum of 6 voltage fault clearing attempts within a 90-second period.

Evaluation AND COMPLIANCE 
These ride-through capabilities will be evaluated during the generator interconnection study processes and load addition study processes outlined in the SPP Open Access Transmission Tariff, and verified through modeling, commissioning tests, or operational monitoring as appropriate.
Compliance mechanisms will be determined through stakeholder discussions, and may include:
Dynamic load modeling validation.
Ride-through performance demonstration.
Data retention and performance logs following disturbance events.

A.1Rationale for Specific ERCOT Voltage Ride-through Requirements 
Allowance for Temporary Active power Reduction in Area B
Reducing consumption during voltage sags can help improve voltage recovery.
It is crucial that HILL is restored as quickly as possible once voltage recovers.
LVRT Curve – Potential Load Disconnection in Area C
Voltage < 0.5 pu for 9 cycles (~150 ms): Loads near the fault may experience deeper voltage sags but should ride through normally cleared faults (4–7 cycles). Exceptions may be needed for delayed clearing under deep voltage sag conditions.
Voltage between 0.5 pu and 0.8 pu for 0.5 seconds: Loads further from the fault may experience voltage sags in this range and should ride through delayed fault clearing, which could last up to 30 cycles (0.5 seconds).
